## What are the canonical formulas per Damodaran, McKinsey, Greenwald, Mauboussin?

### Takeaway
Damodaran's own data-page definitions give a usable, consistent formula set (FCFF, FCFE, ROC, invested capital, reinvestment rate, EVA, WACC, unlevered beta) that ties together as growth = reinvestment rate x ROC. McKinsey's value driver formula, Greenwald's EPV/franchise value, Mauboussin's price-implied expectations, and the Ohlson residual income model are additions the platform does not yet have, and each can be built from the same XBRL inputs.

### Cited Findings

**Damodaran definitions (from his data "variable definitions" page; note this page may be older than the January 2026 data, since it still cites a fixed 5.5% risk premium)**
- Enterprise value = Market value of equity + Market value of debt - Cash. Leases and minority interest are not mentioned in the definition. Source: [Damodaran variable definitions](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/variable.htm)
- EBITDA = EBIT + depreciation and amortization; EV/EBITDA = (Market value of equity + debt - cash) / EBITDA, with book debt used as a proxy for market debt. Source: [Damodaran variable definitions](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/variable.htm)
- ROC = EBIT(1-t) / (BV of debt + BV of equity - cash); invested capital = BV equity + BV debt - cash. Source: [Damodaran variable definitions](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/variable.htm)
- Reinvestment rate = (Net capex + change in WC) / EBIT(1-t); net capex = capex - depreciation; non-cash working capital = current assets excluding cash - current liabilities excluding interest-bearing debt. Source: [Damodaran variable definitions](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/variable.htm)
- FCFF = EBIT(1-t) - (Capex - Depreciation) - change in non-cash WC. Source: [Damodaran variable definitions](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/variable.htm)
- FCFE = Net income - (Capex - Depreciation) - change in non-cash WC - (Principal repaid - New debt issued). Source: [Damodaran variable definitions](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/variable.htm)
- EVA = (Return on capital - Cost of capital) x BV of capital. Source: [Damodaran variable definitions](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/variable.htm)
- WACC = Cost of equity x E/(D+E) + after-tax cost of debt x D/(D+E), with market-value weights; pre-tax cost of debt = risk-free rate + default spread (spread estimated from 5-year stock price standard deviation); tax rate = effective rate (taxes paid / taxable income). Source: [Damodaran variable definitions](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/variable.htm)
- Beta: US firms use weekly returns vs NYSE Composite over 5 years (minimum 2 years); unlevered beta = Beta / (1 + (1 - t) x D/E). Source: [Damodaran variable definitions](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/variable.htm)
- Fundamental growth: growth in operating income = reinvestment rate x return on capital; when ROC changes, expected growth = ROC(t+1) x reinvestment rate + (ROC(t+1) - ROC(t)) / ROC(t). Worked example: Disney 53.93% x 12.61% = 6.8%. EPS growth cannot exceed ROE in the long term. Source: [Damodaran valuation slides, session 8](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/valonlineslides/session8.pdf); [Damodaran valuation session 9 slides](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/podcasts/valspr20/session9slides.pdf)
- Damodaran does not add back stock-based compensation when computing FCFE and calls adding SBC back to earnings "indefensible"; he treats SBC as closer to in-kind compensation than a non-cash charge like depreciation. Source: [Damodaran blog, Feb 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/02/stock-based-employee-compensation-value.html); [Damodaran FCF primer, Oct 2022](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/10/earnings-and-cash-flows-primer-on-free.html)

**McKinsey "Valuation" (Koller et al.)**
- Value driver formula: Value = NOPAT(t+1) x (1 - g/RONIC) / (WACC - g); RONIC (return on new invested capital) can be lower than ROIC on existing capital; recommended by Koller et al. (2010) for continuing value. Original book text not retrieved; attribution from secondary sources. Source: [Jennergren technical note (SSE)](https://research.hhs.se/esploro/outputs/workingPaper/Value-Driver-Formulas-for-Continuing-Value/991001535499306056); [Engineering Economist 2013 via IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/uteexx/v58y2013i1p59-70.html)
- Critiques: Jennergren argues the formula has weaknesses and the plain Gordon formula gives the same result; Bodmer argues it overstates value when RONIC < ROIC and growth changes. Source: [Jennergren](https://research.hhs.se/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Technical-Note-Value-Driver-Formulas-for/991001480420106056); [Bodmer](https://edbodmer.com/mckinsey-value-driver-formula-distortions/)

**Mauboussin / Counterpoint Global**
- Counterpoint primer "Return on Invested Capital: How to Calculate ROIC and Handle Common Issues" (October 2022) defines ROIC = NOPAT / invested capital and computes it two ways: traditional (as reported) and adjusted to treat some intangible investment as capital; the intangible adjustment tends to pull ROICs toward the average. Full PDF returned HTTP 403 to my fetch, so details are from search excerpts. Source: [Counterpoint ROIC primer (Eaton Vance copy)](https://www.eatonvance.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_returnoninvestedcapital.pdf)
- ROIC persistence: secondary summaries of Mauboussin's 2007 "Death, Taxes, and Reversion to the Mean" report 41% of top-quintile-ROIC companies in 1997 were still top quintile nine years later (vs 20% if random). "Measuring the Moat" (2024) found ROICs reverted substantially for 2013-2023 US companies. Source: [GuruFocus summary](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/19462/mauboussin-on-strategy-roic-patterns-luck-persistence-and-what-to-do-about-it); [Measuring the Moat (Morgan Stanley)](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/publication/insights/articles/article_measuringthemoat.pdf); critique of start/end-only method at [req.no](https://req.no/2022/03/roic-persistence)
- Expectations Investing (Mauboussin and Rappaport, revised 2021): start from the price and solve for the expectations it implies (reverse DCF), then compare to your own view. The "market-implied forecast period" is the number of years of value-creating growth needed to justify the price. The authors' site offers a free reverse DCF spreadsheet. Source: [expectationsinvesting.com](https://expectationsinvesting.com/); [book page](https://www.expectationsinvesting.com/the-book); [CFA Institute blog](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/?p=24319)
- A secondhand forum paraphrase says the authors place typical implied forecast periods between 5 and 15 years (range 0 to 30). Unverified against the book. Source: [Motley Fool discussion board](https://discussion.fool.com/t/expectations-investing-question/74971)
- Counterpoint also published "Market-Expected Return on Investment" (MEROI); PDF returned 403, so I could not extract its formula. Source: [Morgan Stanley PDF](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/publication/insights/articles/article_marketexpectedreturnoninvestment_en.pdf)

**Greenwald EPV and franchise value**
- EPV = adjusted (sustainable) earnings / cost of capital, i.e. a no-growth perpetuity; reproduction value is what a competitor would need to spend to replicate the assets; franchise value = EPV - asset (reproduction) value; EPV = asset value implies no competitive advantage. Sources are secondary (lecture notes and blogs), not Greenwald's book. Source: [Kellogg practicum lecture notes](https://kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/jagannathan/teaching/practicum/2006/files/Week3/VI_Lecture_3.pdf); [Old School Value](https://www.oldschoolvalue.com/?p=2317); [Stockopedia](https://stockopedia.com/content/how-does-the-earnings-power-valuation-technique-epv-work-60553)
- Lecture-note guidance: "when in doubt discount franchise value." Source: [Kellogg lecture notes](https://kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/jagannathan/teaching/practicum/2006/files/Week3/VI_Lecture_3.pdf)

**Residual income (Edwards-Bell-Ohlson)**
- Ohlson (1995), "Earnings, Book Value, and Dividends in Equity Valuation," Contemporary Accounting Research 11 (Spring): 661-687. Value = book value + PV of expected abnormal earnings (earnings minus a capital charge on opening book value). Source: [Exeter repository paper citing Ohlson 1995](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/rest/bitstreams/87970/retrieve); [Mongolian journal summary](https://journal.num.edu.mn/accounting/article/download/6787/6016/12977)
- Feltham and Ohlson (1995) show that under clean-surplus accounting, market value = book value + NPV of expected abnormal earnings. Source: [Feltham-Ohlson 1995 PDF](https://u.osu.edu/schroeder.9/files/2017/07/Feltham1995-1kngbjq.pdf)
- Ohlson (2001) revisits the 1995 model and says the RIV model is not the crucial centerpiece of the analysis. Source: [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:coacre:v:18:y:2001:i:1:p:107-120)

**Implied cost of capital**
- Gebhardt, Lee and Swaminathan (2001), "Toward an Implied Cost of Capital," JAR 39(1): 135-176: solve the residual income model for the discount rate that equates it to the market price, using analyst forecasts; firm characteristics (industry, B/M, long-term growth forecasts, forecast dispersion) explain around 60% of cross-sectional variation in two-year-ahead implied cost of capital. Source: [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v39y2001i1p135-176.html); [Kellogg research page](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2001/toward-an-implied-cost-of-capital)
- Easton and Sommers (2007) criticize the approach because growth assumptions beyond the short forecast horizon are questionable (noted in an NBER paper). Source: [NBER w15950](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15950.pdf)

**Other screens**
- Graham number = sqrt(22.5 x EPS x BVPS), where 22.5 = 15 (max P/E) x 1.5 (max P/B). Input choice (trailing vs average EPS) is not settled across sources. Source: [YCharts glossary](https://ycharts.com/glossary/terms/graham_number); [Investing.com Academy](https://www.investing.com/academy/analysis/benjamin-graham-number-definition)
- Rule of 40 = revenue growth % + profit margin % >= 40; sources disagree on margin (EBITDA margin vs FCF margin). Claimed McKinsey statistics about how many SaaS firms meet it conflict across secondary sites and are unverified. Source: [CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/valuation/rule-of-40); [Wall Street Prep](https://wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/rule-of-40); [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/saas-rule-of-40)

**Accruals (Sloan 1996) and related evidence**
- Sloan (1996), "Do Stock Prices Fully Reflect Information in Accruals and Cash Flows About Future Earnings?", The Accounting Review 71(3): 289-315. Investors act as if they "fixate" on earnings; a long low-accrual / short high-accrual decile hedge earns abnormal returns. A secondary source reports roughly 10% in year one, then about 5% and 3% in the next two years (scaled by average total assets). Exact figures not verified against the original. Source: [Sloan 1996 PDF (CUHK copy)](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/acy2/workshop/June2009Wasley/1996TAR).pdf); [Rajgopal et al., Columbia](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/12943/Rajgopal_why_accrual_anomaly.pdf)
- Balance-sheet accruals as implemented in the FAR book (Sloan style): Accruals = (dCA - dCash) - (dCL - dSTD - dTaxesPayable) - D&A, scaled by average total assets. Source: [Gow and Ding, Financial Accounting Research, ch. 15](https://iangow.github.io/far_book/accruals.html)
- Hribar and Collins (2002, JAR 40(1)) show balance-sheet accruals are contaminated by non-articulation events (M&A, divestitures) and that the cash-flow version (net income - operating cash flow) is preferable; mispricing tests using balance-sheet accruals misclassify extreme-accrual firms. Source: [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:joares:v:40:y:2002:i:1:p:105-134); [FAR book](https://iangow.github.io/far_book/accruals.html)
- The anomaly appears to have weakened: Green, Hand and Soliman (2011), "Going, going, gone? The apparent demise of the accruals anomaly," Management Science 57: 797-816. Source: [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v57y2011i5p797-816.html)
- Later work attributes much of Sloan's result to inventory, receivables and payables accruals. Source: [Rajgopal et al.](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/12943/Rajgopal_why_accrual_anomaly.pdf); [NTU inventory paper](https://www3.ntu.edu.sg/home/HuaiZhang/inventory%20paper-RAST.pdf)
- Novy-Marx (2013), "The Other Side of Value: The Gross Profitability Premium," JFE 108(1): 1-28: gross profits / assets predicts cross-sectional returns about as well as book-to-market; profitable firms earn higher returns despite higher valuations; controlling for gross profitability explains most earnings-related anomalies. Source: [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:108:y:2013:i:1:p:1-28); [NBER w15940](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15940)

### Inferences
- Formula set to standardize in `lib/` (my synthesis of the cited definitions; tag names in "Gaps"/Q3 caveats):
  - FCFF = EBIT(1-t) - (capex - D&A) - dNWC, or equivalently CFO + after-tax interest - capex. Fundamental growth g = reinvestment rate x ROIC, so FCFF = NOPAT x (1 - g/ROIC). This last identity is the same as the McKinsey value driver and is a good consistency check on the DCF workbench: if the user's growth and margin inputs imply a reinvestment rate that is negative or above 100%, flag it.
  - FCFE = CFO - capex + net borrowing. SBC is not added back (Damodaran).
  - SBC-adjusted FCF = CFO - capex - ShareBasedCompensation (CFO already adds SBC back under US GAAP indirect method, so subtracting it reverses the add-back). This is the practical "owner" FCF for tech names.
  - EVA / economic profit = (ROIC - WACC) x invested capital (beginning or average). Economic profit = NOPAT - WACC x IC is the same thing.
  - EPV (Greenwald) = normalized EBIT x (1-t) / WACC (plus excess cash, minus debt for equity value). Franchise value = EPV - reproduction value; a free-data proxy for reproduction value is book invested capital, clearly labeled as a proxy.
  - Residual income value = BV0 + sum[(ROE_t - r) x BV_(t-1) / (1+r)^t] + terminal. Implied cost of capital (GLS) = the r that solves this equation at today's price. Both can reuse the existing reverse-DCF solver.
  - Implied growth from P/E: under a Gordon-type model P/E = payout x (1+g)/(r - g) so g = (r x P/E - payout) / (P/E + payout) for trailing P/E. PEG = (P/E) / (growth in %). Reverse DCF is the more general version.
  - Accrual ratio (cash-flow version, preferred per Hribar-Collins) = (NetIncomeLoss - NetCashProvidedByUsedInOperatingActivities) / average Assets. Keep the balance-sheet version only as a secondary.
  - Gross profitability (Novy-Marx) = GrossProfit / Assets: cheap to add and empirically supported.
- New for PIF (not in the current feature list): EPV/franchise value, EVA spread bars, residual income/implied cost of capital, market-implied forecast period, gross profitability, cash-flow accruals, SBC-adjusted FCF, Rule of 40 for software holdings, fundamental growth check.

### Gaps
- McKinsey "Valuation" (7th/8th ed.) primary text was not retrieved; ROIC/NOPAT definitions (EBITA-based NOPAT, operating vs financing invested capital, goodwill inclusion) are standard McKinsey practice but uncited here.
- Mauboussin ROIC primer and MEROI paper returned HTTP 403; exact Counterpoint NOPAT and invested-capital adjustments (e.g. lease and goodwill treatment) not extracted.
- Greenwald's own text not retrieved; EPV adjustments (maintenance capex vs depreciation, cyclical normalization, excess SG&A for growth) are described only by secondary sources.
- No primary source found for the exact Sloan hedge-return figures or for SBC-adjusted FCF predictive evidence.
- Damodaran's variable-definitions page may lag his 2026 methodology (it still says 5.5% ERP).

## How does Damodaran compute and publish implied ERP and industry WACC/betas, and what other ERP / risk-free sources exist (with cost)?

### Takeaway
Damodaran publishes a free monthly S&P 500 implied ERP (4.41% on November 1, 2026, trailing-12-month cash yield with adjusted payout) plus free annual industry betas/WACC/EV multiples/EVA/lease-effect files updated each January (last January 9, 2026, next January 2027). Kroll's US recommendation is 5.0% ERP with a normalized 3.5% or spot 20-year Treasury (whichever is higher); Kroll's Navigator is paid with no public price. FRED DGS10 is free and daily (5.10% on 2026-11-12).

### Cited Findings
- Implied ERP on November 1, 2026 = 4.41% (trailing 12 month, with adjusted payout); alternatives 3.79% (trailing 12m cash yield), 5.81% (average CF yield last 10 years), 3.43% (net cash yield), 3.39% (normalized earnings and payout). Spreadsheet: ERPNov26.xlsx. Source: [Damodaran home page](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/home.htm); [ERPNov26.xlsx](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pc/implprem/ERPNov26.xlsx)

### Inferences
- Default WACC inputs for PIF: risk-free = FRED DGS10 (already a data source); ERP = Damodaran's current monthly implied ERP (show 4.41% as of 2026-11-01 with the alternatives as a dropdown); show Kroll 5.0% + max(3.5%, DGS20) as an alternative "practitioner" setting. Beta: Damodaran industry unlevered beta relevered at the company's market D/E (bottom-up beta), which is more stable than a single-stock regression.
- The ERP is a monthly-changing input, so the app should store it with an "as of" date and not hard-code it. Scraping the home page line is fragile; downloading the monthly ERPmmmYY.xlsx follows a predictable filename pattern (inference from ERPNov26.xlsx), but that pattern is not documented.
- Damodaran's industry files are refreshed once a year, so peer-median multiples from them go stale by year-end; the platform's own peer comps (live) are fresher, and Damodaran's files are best as a sanity band.

## Which inputs are hard to get from free XBRL (leases, minority interest, segments), and how should they be handled? (including the app's debt and FCF mismatches)

### Takeaway
SEC companyfacts is free, real-time (XBRL APIs update in under a minute) and keyless, but only standard-taxonomy, entity-wide facts are included, 10-Q cash-flow facts are year-to-date, and leases, noncontrolling interest and preferred stock each have tagging quirks. The app's current tag map omits leases, minority interest, preferred, equity investments and SBC, and its FCF code treats missing capex as zero; both are likely contributors to the debt and FCF mismatches.

### Cited Findings
- EDGAR APIs: companyfacts `https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK##########.json`, companyconcept and frames endpoints; no API key; XBRL APIs update in under a minute; nightly bulk companyfacts.zip around 3:00 a.m. ET. Source: [SEC EDGAR API docs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)
- Frames return one fact per entity per calendar period (last filed), with annual = 365 +/- 30 days, quarterly = 91 +/- 30 days, instant = CY####Q#I; fiscal periods are aligned to the nearest calendar period. Only non-custom taxonomies applied to the whole entity are included (so company-specific extension tags and segment-level facts are absent). Source: [SEC EDGAR API docs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)
- Operating lease liability tags: OperatingLeaseLiability, OperatingLeaseLiabilityCurrent, OperatingLeaseLiabilityNoncurrent; XBRL US rule DQC.US.0087 governs balance sheet location disclosure when disaggregated. Finance lease liabilities are often embedded in other balance sheet lines, which is why DQC.US.0124 requires an extensible list identifying the line item and DQC.US.0131 checks totals. Source: [XBRL US DQC_0087](https://xbrl.us/data-rule/dqc_0087/); [DQC_0124](https://xbrl.us/data-rule/dqc_0124/); [DQC_0131](https://xbrl.us/data-rule/dqc_0131/)
- Noncontrolling interest: companies with NCI should report both StockholdersEquity and StockholdersEquityIncludingPortionAttributableToNoncontrollingInterest; MinorityInterest is the element for equity not attributable to the parent (instant, credit). Definitions seen are filer-hosted copies of the taxonomy. Source: [XBRL US NCI guidance](https://xbrl.us/guidance/specific-non-controlling-interest-elements/); [Intel XBRL doc example](https://www.intc.com/filings-reports/annual-reports/xbrl_doc_only/2803)
- Damodaran's lease-effect file (Jan 2026, all US firms) shows lease debt is 4.69% of total debt overall but 10.02% excluding financials, and capitalizing leases moves aggregate ROIC from 10.08% to 9.60% (non-financials 18.92% to 17.39%). He reports both "Lease Debt (Accounting)" and "Lease Debt (My Estimate)". Source: [Damodaran leaseeffect.html](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/leaseeffect.html)
- Hribar and Collins: balance-sheet working-capital changes are distorted by acquisitions/divestitures, so cash-flow-statement figures are preferable for accruals and working-capital changes. Source: [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:joares:v:40:y:2002:i:1:p:105-134)
- Damodaran: do not add back SBC. Source: [Damodaran FCF primer](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/10/earnings-and-cash-flows-primer-on-free.html)

### Inferences
- Debt fix (the Yahoo "total debt includes leases" issue): build two explicit numbers from XBRL instead of one ambiguous one.
  - Financial debt = LongTermDebtNoncurrent + LongTermDebtCurrent (or LongTermDebt if only the total is reported) + ShortTermBorrowings / CommercialPaper. Watch double counting: the app's current `shortTermDebt` list mixes LongTermDebtCurrent, DebtCurrent and ShortTermBorrowings, and `pick()` merges tags per period with the latest filing winning, so a company that reports DebtCurrent (which may include current LTD plus short-term borrowings) and LongTermDebtCurrent can get either one depending on filing order rather than a sum. Verify per company.
  - Lease debt = OperatingLeaseLiability (or Current + Noncurrent) + FinanceLeaseLiability (or Current + Noncurrent). Show "Net debt ex-leases" and "Net debt incl. leases" side by side. Use the incl.-leases version only with lease-adjusted EBITDA (add back operating lease cost, tag OperatingLeaseCost) to stay consistent; mixing lease-inclusive debt with unadjusted EBITDA overstates EV/EBITDA. Under ASC 842 operating lease cost sits inside operating expenses, so EV/EBIT is less distorted than EV/EBITDA.
- EV build: EV = market cap (diluted shares x price) + financial debt (+ lease debt, matched to the multiple) + preferred (PreferredStockValue, plus a temporary-equity tag such as TemporaryEquityCarryingAmountAttributableToParent for redeemable preferred) + noncontrolling interest (MinorityInterest) - cash and short-term investments (CashAndCashEquivalentsAtCarryingValue + ShortTermInvestments / MarketableSecuritiesCurrent; long-term marketable securities such as MarketableSecuritiesNoncurrent are material for mega-cap tech) - equity-method investments (EquityMethodInvestments) when the multiple's denominator excludes their income. Each tag must be verified against the FASB taxonomy viewer (https://xbrlview.fasb.org) since I sourced only some of them. This is the natural input for an EV bridge waterfall.
- FCF mismatch for tech holdings (7 of 9): likely causes visible in the code plus definitional differences:
  1. `lib/fundamentals.js` line 50 uses `orZero(capex)`, so a missing capex tag yields FCF = CFO (overstated). Should be null, or fall back through more tags.
  2. Capex tag coverage: only PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment and PaymentsToAcquireProductiveAssets are mapped. Some companies tag capex with other elements (for example a combined PP&E-and-intangibles payment tag or a company extension, which companyfacts drops). Add PaymentsToAcquireIntangibleAssets as an optional component, and check the filing's own label.
  3. Quarterly cash flows: 10-Q cash-flow statements are year-to-date, and `durationPeriod()` keeps only 75-105 day spans, so Q2, Q3 and Q4 cash-flow quarters are dropped (only Q1 and FY survive). TTM FCF needs YTD differencing (Q4 = FY - 9M YTD).
  4. Company-defined FCF differs: some firms subtract finance-lease principal (RepaymentsOfLongTermCapitalLeaseObligations / FinanceLeasePrincipalPayments) or property acquired under finance leases. The app should show "FCF (CFO - capex)" as the standard and note when the company's own definition differs, rather than trying to match each company.
  5. SBC: show FCF and SBC-adjusted FCF (subtract ShareBasedCompensation) side by side; for tech this gap is often the largest single difference.
- Segments: companyfacts excludes dimensional (segment) facts, so segment revenue/operating income need the filing's XBRL instance or the financial statement data sets; treat as out of scope for v1 or parse from the 10-K.
- Interest expense: the app already falls back InterestExpense -> InterestExpenseNonoperating; some filers report net interest only, which breaks cost-of-debt estimates. Use a synthetic rating (interest coverage to spread) only when interest expense is present.

### Gaps
- I did not verify, per tag, the exact current us-gaap element names for preferred stock, equity-method investments, finance lease principal payments, OperatingLeaseCost or ShareBasedCompensation (vs AllocatedShareBasedCompensationExpense) against the FASB taxonomy; they are listed from working knowledge and need checking at xbrlview.fasb.org.
- I did not run the app against the 9 tech holdings, so the FCF causes above are hypotheses from reading `lib/sources/sec.js` and `lib/fundamentals.js`, not confirmed diagnoses.
- No source found on how often companyfacts' "latest filed wins" dedupe picks a restated vs original value.

## What visualizations are standard?

### Takeaway
The football field (horizontal range bars by method, with the 52-week range as a reference bar and the current price as a vertical line) is the standard summary; sensitivity heatmaps, ROIC-vs-WACC spread over time, EV bridge waterfalls, value driver trees and price-implied expectations charts are the common supporting views. Only the football field had solid sourcing in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Football field: horizontal bar chart of implied value ranges (low/mid/high) from comps, precedents and DCF on one axis; overlap is the most defensible range; the 52-week range is a reference bar, not a method; all market inputs should share one as-of date; offer price drawn as a vertical line. Source: [FE Training](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/valuation/football-field-analysis/); [Ryan O'Connell](https://ryanoconnellfinance.com/football-field-valuation/); [Acadifi](https://www.acadifi.com/community/football-field-valuation-chart-methodology-cfa-level-2)
- Mauboussin's Expectations Investing method centers on comparing price-implied expectations to the analyst's own forecast. Source: [expectationsinvesting.com](https://expectationsinvesting.com/)

### Inferences
- Suggested views for PIF, each mapped to a metric above:
  - Football field per holding: bars for DCF (sensitivity grid min/max), EV/EBITDA and EV/EBIT peer quartiles, EPV (no-growth floor), residual income, Graham number, 52-week range; vertical line at current price.
  - Sensitivity heatmap (already exists): add a contour or outline for the cell equal to current price so it doubles as a reverse DCF.
  - ROIC vs WACC spread bars by year (green above zero, red below) with EVA dollars as a secondary line; benchmark band from Damodaran industry ROIC.
  - Implied expectations chart: price-implied revenue growth (or market-implied forecast period) vs historical growth and consensus.
  - EV bridge waterfall: market cap + debt + leases + preferred + NCI - cash - investments = EV, so members see exactly what drives the multiple and where Yahoo and XBRL differ.
  - Value driver tree: price -> EV -> NOPAT x (1 - g/RONIC) / (WACC - g), with growth, margin, tax rate, capital turnover and WACC as leaves.
  - EPV vs asset value vs market cap stacked bar to show franchise value and growth value (Greenwald's decomposition).

### Gaps
- I found no primary practitioner source (McKinsey, Counterpoint) showing their standard chart forms for ROIC-WACC spread, value driver trees or implied-expectations charts in this pass; those suggestions are inferences.

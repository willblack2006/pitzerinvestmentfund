# Nine tech holdings, one AI capex bet

As of 2026-11-12, the Pitzer Investment Fund's nine technology holdings are less diversified than they look: six of them (NVDA, MU, CRWV, ORCL, P and PLTR) depend in different ways on the same AI infrastructure spending cycle, and two of them (CRWV, ORCL) are the borrowing-heavy landlords at the end of that chain. The names fall into three groups. Cash-rich, profitable businesses where the debate is price and durability (NVDA, MU, PLTR, DUOL, P); debt-funded GPU landlords where the debate is financing (CRWV, ORCL); and small, speculative or distressed positions where the debate is survival and capital allocation (QBTS, MAPS). The fund app's Yahoo data misstates "total debt" for most holdings by counting leases as borrowings (it shows CoreWeave at $66.2B when borrowed debt is $46.9B, and it shows debt for Duolingo, Everpure and WM Technology that is only leases, with Palantir's likely the same), and its free cash flow figures disagree with company-reported numbers for at least seven of the nine. At the app's 2026-11-12 prices, the nine positions cost $15,062 and are worth about $15,803 (+4.9%, derived), with Palantir's $1,522 gain covering losses elsewhere and D-Wave carrying the largest cost basis and the largest dollar loss. Two dated events will make parts of this report stale within days: Nvidia reports on 2026-11-17, five days after its CEO resigned, and Everpure reports between 2026-11-18 and 2026-11-25. This report gives no buy or sell recommendations; the stance is the fund's decision.

## Reading guide and sourcing rules

Items dated before 2026-05-12 are flagged "older than 6 months." Prices are the fund app's Yahoo pull on 2026-11-12 unless stated, and the app's Yahoo prices themselves were not independently checked for every stock. ## CoreWeave (CRWV): a $132.5B backlog carried on $46.9B of borrowed debt

### One-line thesis
CoreWeave is the largest independent GPU landlord with demand locked into multi-year contracts, and its equity value depends on whether credit markets keep funding a build-out that used $17.9B more cash than it generated in the first nine months of 2026 (derived).

### 1. How does the company make money?
CoreWeave rents Nvidia GPU capacity, mostly under multi-year committed contracts. Q3 2026 revenue was **$3,661M, up 168%** from $1,365M, and revenue backlog reached **about $132.5B at 2026-09-30, up 138%** from $55.6B ([CoreWeave Q3 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001769628/000176962826000461/coreweave3q26earningspress.htm)). The 10-Q puts unsatisfied RPO at $132.3B, with 36% expected within 24 months and 38% in months 25 to 48 ([Q3 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001769628/000176962826000465/crwv-20260930.htm)). Concentration has fallen sharply but remains high: anonymized Customer A was 24% of Q3 revenue (67% a year earlier) and Customer B was 30% ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001769628/000176962826000465/crwv-20260930.htm)). Microsoft was about 67% of FY2025 revenue (older than 6 months), so Customer A is very likely Microsoft, though the 10-Q does not name it ([sec-api.io](https://sec-api.io/insights/coreweave-crwv-2026-revenue-analysis-top-customer-concentration-exposure)). Pricing per management is above $40M per MW per year for short-term capacity and $20M to $25M per MW for four- to five-year contracts ([Q3 call transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-coreweave-tops-q3-2026-forecast-and-shares-rise-57-93CH-5017354)).

### 2. What do they sell, and what is the sentiment?
The product is large Nvidia clusters plus a managed software layer: CoreWeave brought multi-rack Vera Rubin NVL72 online, has **over 1.7 GW active**, expects more than 1.85 GW at year-end and has over 4.6 GW contracted ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001769628/000176962826000461/coreweave3q26earningspress.htm)). Sentiment before Q3 was close to distressed. The stock was about $47, down about 40% in a month and more than 73% from a June 2025 peak above $183 ([CNBC, 2026-11-10](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/coreweave-earnings-preview-debt-capex.html)). On 2026-10-14 it fell 15.5%, and the CEO wrote that the market "misunderstands and acts irrationally" ([CNBC, 2026-10-14](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/coreweave-stock-bond-yields-ai-debt.html)). In early November, Bloomberg and CNBC reported Microsoft plans to use fewer neoclouds, per secondary summaries ([Runtimewire](https://runtimewire.com/article/microsoft-cut-neocloud-roster-few-key-suppliers)). The Q3 beat produced a relief rally of about 8% after hours ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/crwv-stock-climbs-6-after-hours-as-132-b-revenue-backlog-reinforces-ai-compute-demand-nbis-iren-tick-higher/cNGuwW0R9JL)). The demand story is working; the stock's problem is financing.

### 3. Who are the competitors, and what is the sentiment around them?
Oracle's OCI posted $7.4B of IaaS revenue (+121%) in the quarter ended August 2026, roughly twice CoreWeave's quarterly revenue ([Oracle FQ1 2027 release](https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/q1fy27-earnings-release-2026-09-10/)). Nebius reported Q2 2026 revenue of $582.3M (+454%) with a 40.6% adjusted EBITDA margin ([The Energy Mag](https://theenergymag.com/news/market-news/nebius-group-reports-454-revenue-growth-in-q2-2026-raises-power-guidance-to-5-gw)). Sentiment across neoclouds is poor: six listed neoclouds fell about 20.9% on average from 2026-10-30 to 2026-11-05 ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-neocloud-stocks-fell-20-9-in-four-days-s-p-500-fell-3/)), and Gartner projected more than 80% of neocloud providers could stop operating independently by 2030 ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/20/gartner-80-of-neocloud-firms-face-extinction-by-2030-as-ai-capex-outpaces-returns/)). The bond market separates the two big landlords: Oracle's 2032 bond yielded 7.24% against about 14.8% on CoreWeave's ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/coreweave-stock-bond-yields-ai-debt.html)).

### 4. How are financials growing compared to competitors?
Growth and EBITDA are strong; GAAP profit is thin. Q3 operating income was $207M (6% margin) against **$846M of net interest expense**, producing a $709M net loss; adjusted EBITDA was $2,256M (62%) ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001769628/000176962826000461/coreweave3q26earningspress.htm)). Interest is about 23% of revenue and GAAP operating income covered it only about 0.24x (derived). FY2026 revenue guidance was raised to $13.0B to $13.3B and exit-2026 run-rate revenue to $20B to $21B ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001769628/000176962826000461/coreweave3q26earningspress.htm)). Management expects adjusted net income to turn positive only in 1H 2028 ([call transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-coreweave-tops-q3-2026-forecast-and-shares-rise-57-93CH-5017354)). CoreWeave grows slower than Nebius from a roughly 6x larger base with a higher EBITDA margin (62% vs about 40%). One caution from the notes: D&A ($1,889M) exceeds reported cost of revenue ($1,183M), so the roughly 67% "gross margin" appears to exclude most GPU depreciation and is not comparable to a hyperscaler's (derived, not checked against the 10-Q line items).

### 5. Cash vs debt
At 2026-09-30 CoreWeave held $4,122M of cash and $62M of marketable securities (plus $2,282M restricted), against recourse debt of $41.6B, non-recourse debt of $5.3B, $18.7B of operating leases and $4,025M of equity ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001769628/000176962826000461/coreweave3q26earningspress.htm)). Borrowed debt is **$46.9B**, up from about $21.4B at 2025-12-31 (derived from the [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001769628/000176962826000465/crwv-20260930.htm)). Nine-month operating cash flow of $5,638M against $23,558M of PP&E purchases gives FCF of about -$17.9B (derived). Two credit tiers exist: weighted average cost of debt is 7.9%, down 250 bps, and the next delayed-draw term loan is expected to price investment grade at about 7% to 7.5% ([call transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-coreweave-tops-q3-2026-forecast-and-shares-rise-57-93CH-5017354)), while the unsecured 9.75% 2031 notes yielded about 15.5% and five-year CDS was about 1,152 bps on 2026-11-10 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/coreweave-earnings-preview-debt-capex.html)). S&P Global Market Intelligence treats CDS at 1,000 bps or above as generally signaling distress ([CNBC, 2026-10-15](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/coreweave-credit-default-swaps-hit-distress-levels-as-investors-sour-on-ai-debt.html)). S&P rates the company B+. Wells Fargo estimated 2027 capex could reach $66B, requiring at least $25B more debt ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/coreweave-earnings-preview-debt-capex.html)). An ATM program of up to 35M shares is about 6% potential dilution (derived).

### 6. P/E ratio and what it implies
There is no meaningful P/E: trailing earnings are negative and Yahoo's forward P/E is -32.6. On enterprise value (about $73.9B excluding leases, derived from a $31.2B market cap), the stock trades near 3.6x exit-2026 run-rate revenue and about 8.2x annualized Q3 adjusted EBITDA (derived), and backlog is about 1.8x EV. Those multiples look cheap for 168% growth, but adjusted EBITDA sits before interest and depreciation, the two largest costs. The low multiple says the market treats the equity as a thin slice on top of a large debt stack, not as a claim on the full backlog.

### Street view vs contrarian view
The Street is positive: FactSet's 39 estimates average overweight with a $126 target as of 2026-11-10 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/coreweave-earnings-preview-debt-capex.html)), and Wells Fargo calls it "the highest quality co in the space." The contrarian bull view goes further than the Street on one point: the equity is priced as if financing fails, while secured, contract-backed debt prices near investment grade. Management also claims about 130,000 GPUs come off contract in 2027 and about 170,000 in 2028, and that re-contracting at current prices could add about $4B of annualized revenue in 2027 and nearly $10B including 2028 with no new capex ([call transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-coreweave-tops-q3-2026-forecast-and-shares-rise-57-93CH-5017354)). The contrarian bear view targets accounting and incentives. CoreWeave depreciates GPUs over six years straight-line ([Cape Fear Advisors](https://capefearadvisors.substack.com/p/the-second-contract)), against an Nvidia product cycle Michael Burry describes as two to three years (secondhand, [State of AI](https://www.stateof.ai/compute-explore/gpu-lifetime)). Nvidia is supplier, investor (a $2B purchase at $87.20 in January, about an 11.5% stake) and customer (over $6B of services through 2032), which Bloomberg framed as "circular financing" ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-26/nvidia-invests-another-2-billion-in-coreweave-offers-new-chip), older than 6 months). Co-founders sold about $3.3B of stock since the March 2025 IPO ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/coreweave-co-founders-sell-33b-of-stock-since-march-ipo)). Neither side is settled: the 2027 renewal cohort is the first real test of six-year depreciation, and it is observable.

### What would change the thesis
- The next delayed-draw term loan prices investment grade at about 7% to 7.5% (supports the bull case), or fails to price (confirms the bear case).
- About $9B of customer prepayments and deposits, which the CEO expects in the three months after 2026-11-10, arrive on schedule.
- Active power reaches at least 1.85 GW at year-end 2026 (Wells Fargo expects 1.95 GW).
- Q4 2026 revenue lands in the implied $4.69B to $4.99B range (derived from guidance), with interest inside the $1.05B to $1.15B guide.
- The 2031 notes' yield moves from about 15.5% and CDS moves relative to the 1,000 bps distress line.
- Renewal pricing on the roughly 130,000 GPUs coming off contract in 2027. The next earnings date is not in the notes.

### Unverified or disputed figures
- Price $56.60 and market cap $31.2B (Yahoo, not verified); a single source says the stock closed about $60.77 on 2026-11-11.
- The Q3 debt footnote could not be retrieved; facility-level balances and coupons come from the Q1 10-Q and conflict across sources.
- Customer B's identity; a further $21B Meta expansion (single source).
- Intrator's 2026 insider sales ($907M vs about $627M by source); whether sales were under 10b5-1 plans was not confirmed.
- Burry's depreciation estimate (secondhand).
- Whether the 67% gross margin excludes depreciation (inferred).

## Duolingo (DUOL): users up 29%, bookings up 12%

### One-line thesis
Duolingo traded about 5 points of 2026 bookings growth for user growth, and the bet is now whether a 29% jump in daily users converts into paying subscribers before free AI tutors compress what people will pay.

### 1. How does the company make money?
Subscriptions are 87% of revenue: **$270.0M of $309.0M in Q3 2026, up 18%**, while advertising ($20.8M, -1%), the Duolingo English Test ($9.9M, +2%) and in-app purchases ($6.9M, -38%) were flat or shrinking ([Q3 2026 shareholder letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)). Total bookings were $316.1M (+12%). DAUs reached 64.9M (+29%), MAUs 153.1M (+13%) and paid subscribers 13.1M (+14%) (same letter). Paid penetration is flat at about 8.6% of MAUs versus 8.5% a year earlier (derived), so the new daily users are mostly free so far. Engagement rose: DAU/MAU went from 37.3% to 42.4% (derived), and the 10-Q credits DAU growth "largely" to retention ([Q3 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069692/duol-20260930.htm)).

### 2. What do they sell, and what is the sentiment?
The product is a free, ad-supported app with paid tiers. Duolingo is ending new sales of its premium Max tier and moving unlimited Video Call (AI conversation) into Super for all subscribers by year-end 2026 ([Q3 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)). That simplifies pricing, but it also suggests a separate premium AI tier did not sell on its own. User sentiment is mixed: reviews of the switch from Hearts to Energy were mostly negative per Class Central ([Class Central](https://www.classcentral.com/report/duolingo-breaks-hearts-for-energy/), search snippet only), and a review aggregator logged a June 2026 surge of complaints about notifications, Energy and ads ([Unstar](https://unstar.app/it/events/duolingo-language-chess-complaints-june-2026), low confidence). Investor sentiment improved after Q3: shares rose 19.4% pre-market on 2026-11-04 and Wells Fargo upgraded from Underweight to Equal-Weight, with several targets raised ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/analyst-stock-ratings/price-target/26/11/62864035/duolingo-analysts-boost-their-forecasts-after-better-than-expected-q3-results)).

### 3. Who are the competitors, and what is the sentiment around them?
No language app is close in scale. Chegg, owner of Busuu, reported Q3 2026 revenue of $46.5M, down 40% ([Chegg Q3 2026 release](https://globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/03/3394197/0/en/chegg-reports-third-quarter-2026-earnings.html)), and Speak's last disclosed round valued it at $1B in December 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/10/openai-backed-speak-raises-78m-at-1b-valuation-to-help-users-learn-languages-by-talking-out-loud), older than 6 months). The real competitor is general AI: Google Translate added a free Gemini-powered Practice mode ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/google-translates-latest-feature-is-its-take-on-duolingo-160035157.html)), though Android Authority found it "surprisingly good" but much less structured than Duolingo ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-translate-vs-duolingo-3603240/)). The evidence cuts both ways. DAU growth rose from 21% to 23% to 29% during 2026 as these tools spread, which argues against user loss; Duolingo giving AI features away while paid penetration stays flat argues for pricing pressure. Chegg's collapse is the cautionary case, but Chegg sold homework answers that ChatGPT replaces directly, while Duolingo sells habit.

### 4. How are financials growing compared to competitors?
Duolingo still grows faster than mature consumer subscription peers, at a much higher gross margin. Q3 2026 gross margin was 72.9% and adjusted EBITDA margin 25.5%, down from 29.5% ([Q3 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)). Spotify grew revenue 16% (14% constant currency) with a 33.0% gross margin ([Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-is-on-course-for-a-3-3b-annual-operating-profit-in-2026-after-adding-6m-subscribers-in-q3)), and Match Group's revenue fell about 3% with payers down 5% ([Securities.io](https://www.securities.io/payers-fall-5-at-match-group-as-q3-revenue-slips-to-891-million/)). But Duolingo's own growth fell from 39% in FY2025 to 14% in Q3 2026 ([Q4 2025 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012513/q4fy25duolingo12-31x25shar.htm), older than 6 months). Bookings are reaccelerating: +8% in Q2, +12% in Q3 and +13% guided for Q4. Stock-based compensation is about 14% of revenue, and Q4 gross margin is guided down 90 bps to 72.0% as AI features widen.

### 5. Cash vs debt
The balance sheet is very strong: **$1,484.0M of cash and investments and no borrowings** at 2026-09-30; the only liability resembling debt is an $84.2M long-term operating lease ([Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069692/duol-20260930.htm)). Trailing FCF is about $395M on the company's definition (Q4 2025 $93.7M plus nine-month 2026 $301.8M, derived). Buybacks have been small and price-sensitive: $82.0M of a $400M authorization through 2026-10-30, mostly bought near $100 in the spring, with only $6.4M in Q3 ([Q3 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)).

### 6. P/E ratio and what it implies
Trailing GAAP P/E of about 52 is real: trailing GAAP net income is about $145.8M against a $7.43B market cap (derived), now that the $222.7M one-time tax benefit from Q3 2025 has rolled out ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069692/duol-20260930.htm)). The forward P/E of 20.1 implies forward EPS of about $7.83 (derived), which only fits adjusted EPS that excludes SBC; GAAP EPS is running about $0.54 to $0.66 a quarter. Read the drop from 52x to 20x as a change of accounting basis plus growth, not a doubling of earnings. On EV/FCF (about 15x, derived), the stock looks reasonable only if bookings growth reaccelerates in 2027.

### Street view vs contrarian view
The Street is still neutral: 15 Hold, 8 Buy and 1 Sell out of 24 firms, with a mean target of $141.56 on 2026-11-05 ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/consensus-duolingo-inc-nasdaq-duol-stock-has-consensus-target-price-of-14156-according-to-analysts-2026-11-05/)), at or below the $157.42 price. The contrarian bull view is that the February reset, which gave up more than $50M of bookings to rebuild user growth ([Q4 2025 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012513/q4fy25duolingo12-31x25shar.htm)), worked faster than promised (29% DAU growth against a roughly 20% target) and that revenue lags bookings, so 2027 revenue growth should rise. The contrarian bear view is that the user growth may be cheap to buy: mobile-game partnerships, aggressive notifications and an **$11.4M company-wide bonus accrual tied to Q4 DAU growth of 25% or more** ([Q3 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)) all lift DAUs without lifting payers. In 2025, Sensor Tower estimates showed DAU growth slowing before the company reported it (secondhand, [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/duolingos-100m-dau-blueprint-sacrificing-151000278.html), older than 6 months), and a securities class action filed 2026-10-07 alleges misleading statements about user growth ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069692/duol-20260930.htm)). The stock is about 71% below its May 2025 high above $544 (derived).

### What would change the thesis
- Q4 2026 DAU growth relative to the 25% bonus threshold, and Q4 bookings against the $382M (+13%) guide.
- Paid subscriber growth closing the gap with DAU growth (14% vs 29% in Q3).
- 2027 guidance in February 2027: whether bookings growth moves back toward the "nearly 20%" management says it gave up.
- Q4 gross margin versus the 72.0% guide as Video Call reaches all Super subscribers.
- Lead-plaintiff motions in the class action are due 2026-12-07.

### Unverified or disputed figures
- Forward P/E basis (adjusted vs GAAP) is inferred, not confirmed.
- Yahoo's FCF of $0.31B vs the company's roughly $395M; definition unknown.
- The February 2026 post-guidance closing price conflicts across sources ($113.24 vs other reports).
- Whether part of the 2025 tax benefit sits in Q4 2025, which would push true trailing P/E slightly higher.
- No 2026 Sensor Tower or Similarweb data to check the reported 29% DAU growth independently.
- The 12.7% GAAP operating margin is from the app's Yahoo pull, not verified.

## WM Technology (MAPS): the market prices Weedmaps below its $51M of cash

## Micron (MU): record margins at what may be a cyclical peak

### One-line thesis
Micron is earning the highest margins in memory history on a price-driven boom, and the thesis turns on whether take-or-pay contracts with price floors have broken a cycle in which the stock historically peaked while P/E still looked low.

### 1. How does the company make money?
DRAM (including HBM) was **73% of fiscal Q4 2026 revenue ($39.8B) and NAND 26% ($14.1B)**, on total revenue of $54.2B, up 31% sequentially and 379% year over year ([Q4 FY26 prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Q4-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)). By unit, Cloud Memory (including HBM) was $16.3B, Core Data Center $18.0B, Mobile and Client $13.1B and Automotive and Embedded $6.8B, with every unit at 79% to 88% operating margin ([Q4 FY26 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723125/000072312526000018/a2026q4ex991-pressrelease.htm)). The growth is mostly price: DRAM bits rose mid-single digits while prices rose high-teens percent sequentially, and NAND bits rose about 10% while prices rose about 30% ([prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Q4-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)). Micron did not disclose an HBM dollar figure.

### 2. What do they sell, and what is the sentiment?
Micron sells DRAM, HBM and NAND/SSDs, and has completed agreements for the vast majority of its calendar 2027 HBM supply at significant price increases. It has signed 26 strategic customer agreements, estimated at over 35% of revenue through 2030, three-quarters of which have a defined pricing framework (a majority with floors and ceilings), backed by $32B of customer commitments and about $150B of RPO ([prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Q4-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)). Micron expects supply to stay tight through 2027 and 2028 but a "more moderate rate of price increases" in FY27. TrendForce forecasts 4Q26 conventional DRAM contract prices up 10% to 15% ([TrendForce, 2026-09-30](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260930-13258.html)), below the high-teens rise Micron got in its August quarter. Stock sentiment turned volatile: shares fell 4% on 2026-11-02 after Kioxia's cautious outlook ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/02/micron-falls-4-as-kioxias-cautious-outlook-hits-memory-stocks-western-digital-drops-5-sandisk-slides-3/)), and one report said Micron had lost nearly 30% of its market value in the previous month ([Invezz, 2026-11-09](https://invezz.com/sg/news/2026/11/09/why-are-micron-and-sandisk-stocks-rising-today/)).

### 3. Who are the competitors, and what is the sentiment around them?
SK hynix leads HBM with about 50% share in Q2 2026, Samsung has about 33% and Micron about 18% (Counterpoint, via [Sammy Fans](https://www.sammyfans.com/2026/10/27/samsung-sk-hynix-q3-hbm-revenue-q4-share/)). In total DRAM revenue for Q3 2026, Samsung had 38%, SK hynix 29%, Micron 21% and China's CXMT 9%, up from 5% a year earlier ([PC Testbench](https://www.pctestbench.com/dram-market-share-q3-2026/)). Samsung says its 2027 HBM supply is sold out ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/KR_005930.KS_2026-10-29)), and CXMT raised 66.6B yuan in a July IPO ([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/tech-science/20261113/chinas-memory-makers-are-catching-up-what-happens-when-they-do)). Samsung's HBM catch-up is the main risk to HBM pricing; CXMT's commodity DRAM is the main risk to mobile, PC and auto pricing if China's own demand softens.

### 4. How are financials growing compared to competitors?
All three majors are posting record margins, so Micron does not stand out. Micron's fiscal Q4 GAAP operating margin was 80.7% (82.3% non-GAAP) and FY26 GAAP EPS was $74.33 against $7.59 ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723125/000072312526000018/a2026q4ex991-pressrelease.htm)). SK hynix's Q3 2026 operating margin was about 79.7% per secondary call summaries ([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9150253/sk-hynix-inc-skhy-q3-2026-earnings-call-highlights-record-krw1046-trillion-revenue-and-80-operating-margin-as-hbm4-ramps)), and Samsung's memory revenue rose 427% year over year with a 72% chip-division margin that also includes foundry ([Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202610292152037/)). Similar growth across the group (Micron +379%, Samsung memory +427%) points to an industry price shock, not Micron gaining share. FQ1 FY27 guidance is $61.5B ± $1.5B of revenue, about 86.25% gross margin and $38.15 of non-GAAP EPS ([prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Q4-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)).

### 5. Cash vs debt
Micron holds **$73.5B of cash and investments against $5.2B of debt**, or $68.3B of net cash, and is rated BBB+ or equivalent by all three agencies ([prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Q4-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)). About $12.7B of that cash is customer deposits that must eventually be returned, so net cash excluding deposits is about $55.6B (derived). FY26 adjusted FCF was $62.3B ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723125/000072312526000018/a2026q4ex991-pressrelease.htm)). FY27 capex will rise sharply: about $25B in the first half and more in the second, implying above $50B for the year (derived) versus $30.7B gross in FY26. Inventory rose to 129 days, up 9 days, which management attributes to build-ahead.

### 6. P/E ratio and what it implies
MU trades at **10.7x trailing GAAP EPS** and 3.8x (Yahoo) to 4.52x (StockAnalysis) forward, at 6.53x book ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mu/statistics/)). The forward multiples imply EPS of about $177 to $209 (derived), up from $74. Memory history warns that a low P/E at peak earnings is a trap: in 2018 the stock peaked at 7.3x trailing and fell 56% while earnings were still near peak, and in 2022 it peaked at 13.5x trailing and fell 51% ([Theta Research](https://www.theta.md/research/mu-cycle-peak-anatomy), older than 6 months). Book value is a steadier anchor at a cycle peak, and at about 6.5x book the stock is far above its pre-2026 history.

### Street view vs contrarian view
The Street is strongly positive: 49 analysts rate it "Strong Buy" with a mean target of $1,561.91, about 95.6% above the price ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mu/statistics/)). The contrarian bear view rests on cycle history, record capex and slowing price gains; it also notes that HBM held Cloud Memory margins flat, so HBM is currently margin-dilutive versus conventional DRAM, which cuts against the idea that HBM is the profit engine. Michael Burry's reported short and the stock's distance above its 200-day average are cited by skeptics but are unverified ([Stoxmetrics](https://stoxmetrics.substack.com/p/micron-is-not-priced-for-a-broken)). The contrarian bull view is that this cycle is structurally different: data center is over 57% of revenue versus under 30% in 2018 ([Theta Research](https://www.theta.md/research/mu-cycle-peak-anatomy)), and BofA's bear case, with DRAM and NAND prices down 30% and 40%, still leaves EPS near $100 versus about $12 at the 2018 peak ([Benzinga, Aug 2026](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/08/60911521/micron-bear-case-earnings-memory-downturn)), which would put MU at about 8x trough earnings (derived). The take-or-pay contracts have never been tested in a downturn, and most new capacity (Idaho mid-2027 and late 2028, Japan late 2028) arrives in the same years Micron says supply stays tight.

### What would change the thesis
- FQ1 FY27 results in December 2026 against $61.5B ± $1.5B revenue, 86.25% gross margin (which management calls the FY27 floor) and $38.15 EPS.
- The rate of DRAM and NAND contract price increases: TrendForce's 4Q26 DRAM range of +10% to 15% versus Micron's prior high-teens; a sequential price decline would be the clearest signal.
- Inventory days moving further above 129.
- The buyback step-up planned from 2026-12-09 and its size relative to FY26's $650M.
- Samsung's HBM share (estimates near 40% in Q4) and any customer renegotiating a contract floor.

### Unverified or disputed figures
- Forward P/E (3.8x vs 4.52x) depends on estimate period; neither methodology confirmed.
- Yahoo's FCF of $29.1B does not match any Micron figure.
- TrendForce 4Q26 DRAM forecasts conflict (10% to 15% vs about 20%).
- SK hynix Q3 figures come from secondary call summaries; HBM share data comes via secondary reports.
- "Nearly 30% drop in a month" is a single secondary source.
- Burry short position is unconfirmed.

### Street view vs contrarian view

Management attributes weak bookings to shorter Sovereign AI contracts, which would mean TCV understates demand. With the fund's position up about 8.8x, the evidence frames the decision as "strong business, demanding price"; that is for the fund to weigh.

### What would change the thesis

- Q4 revenue against the $2.649B to $2.653B guide (about +15% sequentially).
- RPO moving from $4.9B and contract durations lengthening on Sovereign AI deals.
- International commercial growth above the 1% sequential rate.
- Procurement scrutiny of non-competitive awards, or budget changes affecting US government revenue.

## D-Wave Quantum (QBTS): a $3.74B bet on $13M of trailing revenue

### One-line thesis
D-Wave has about three years of cash and the largest commercial customer base in quantum, but trailing revenue of about $13M against a $3.74B market cap means the stock prices outcomes in the 2030s, and near-term revenue is falling.

### 1. How does the company make money?
Revenue is tiny and lumpy. Q3 2026 revenue was **$4.5M, up 21%**, with professional services and quantum-computing-as-a-service (QCaaS) up and system sales down; nine-month revenue was $10.4M against $21.8M a year earlier, which included a large system sale ([D-Wave Q3 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001907982/000190798226000180/qbts-20261110xexx991.htm)). Per the call, QCaaS was $2.2M and professional services $2.0M, the latter driven by a $10M two-year Fortune 100 engagement ([gloom.sh call summary](https://gloom.sh/stocks/qbts/transcripts/q3-2026)), so recurring cloud revenue runs at about $9M a year (derived). Q3 bookings fell 19% to $1.9M. Nine-month bookings of $37.4M include a $20M Florida Atlantic University system, with installation revenue "likely in early 2027." TIKR estimates nine-month bookings excluding two January deals and acquired orders were about $5.1M versus $5.3M a year earlier ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/d-wave-sinks-8-as-q3-loss-misses-and-quarterly-bookings-fall-19-after-quantum-rally)).

### 2. What do they sell, and what is the sentiment?
D-Wave sells quantum annealing, an optimization-focused approach, through the cloud and on-premises systems, and is building a gate-model line through the Quantum Circuits acquisition, targeting a first error-corrected logical qubit in 2028 and 100 logical qubits by 2032 ([gloom.sh](https://gloom.sh/stocks/qbts/transcripts/q3-2026)). Its 2025 "beyond-classical" Science claim is contested: Flatiron Institute and Boston University researchers reached comparable accuracy on workstations for targeted problems ([Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/flatiron-institute-tensor-network-algorithm-overturns-historical-d-wave-quantum-supremacy-claim/)), and D-Wave rebuts that the classical work misses hard regimes ([Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/d-wave-systematically-rebuts-flatiron-claims-reaffirming-beyond-classical-simulation-milestones/)). The flagship production customer, NTT DOCOMO, built its apps in-house and runs them infrequently. The stock fell about 8% on Q3 results, and B. Riley cut its target to $27 from $40 on 2026-11-02 with the stock 73% below its 52-week high ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/b-riley-cut-its-d-wave-target-to-27-its-still-168-above-the-stock-price)). A securities class action filed 2026-10-15 alleges record bookings came mostly from two older contracts and that deliveries were slipping (single source, unproven).

### 3. Who are the competitors, and what is the sentiment around them?
The sector is diverging. IonQ reported Q3 2026 revenue of $173.8M (+336%), about half of it foundry services from SkyWater, which it bought on 2026-07-31; it has $3.7B of pro forma cash ([IonQ Q3 release](https://investors.ionq.com/news/news-details/2026/IonQ-Announces-Record-3Q26-Revenues-and-Raises-FY2026-Guidance-to-505---515-Million-as-Backlog-Grows-to-1-2-Billion/default.aspx)). SkyWater is D-Wave's foundry, so a direct rival now owns a plant D-Wave's chip roadmap relies on; D-Wave's CEO said supply chain prudence "requires diversification of suppliers." Rigetti's Q3 revenue was $1.4M, about 60% below expectations ([Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/11/11/rigetti-q3-2026-results-quantum-computing-roadmap/)), and Quantum Computing Inc's $9.5M came mostly from acquisitions ([QuiverQuant](https://www.quiverquant.com/news/Quantum+Computing+QUBT+Reports+%249.5+Million+Third-Quarter+Revenue)). Quantinuum went public around 2026-06-03 at about $14B to $15B on $30.9M of 2025 revenue (aggregator, [Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/exits/quantinuum)).

### 4. How are financials growing compared to competitors?
Revenue is shrinking year to date while costs nearly doubled after the Quantum Circuits deal. Q3 GAAP gross margin fell to 52.9% from 71.4%, GAAP opex rose 84% to $55.9M and the operating loss widened to $53.5M ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001907982/000190798226000180/qbts-20261110xexx991.htm)), an operating margin of about -1,190% (derived). Consensus 2026 revenue of about $42.5M to $42.7M implies about $32M in Q4 ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/d-wave-sinks-8-as-q3-loss-misses-and-quarterly-bookings-fall-19-after-quantum-rally)), which conflicts with management's statement that FAU installation revenue is likely in early 2027. Unless another system is recognized in Q4, FY2026 consensus looks at risk (derived inference). D-Wave's revenue is "purer" quantum than IonQ's foundry-heavy mix, with a higher gross margin, but IonQ has scale.

### 5. Cash vs debt
At 2026-09-30 D-Wave held **$503.4M of cash and securities against $42.0M of loans** ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001907982/000190798226000180/qbts-20261110xexx991.htm)), down from over $884M at year-end 2025, mainly because of the $252.8M Quantum Circuits purchase. Nine-month cash burn (operations plus capex) was about $122M, or about $41M a quarter, implying about three years of runway (derived). No equity was raised in 2026's first nine months, after $536.7M of ATM sales in 2025. A CHIPS agreement of up to $100M pays in exchange for a minority equity stake, reportedly about 7.1M shares at $14.093 ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/d-wave-100m-chips-act-deal/), secondary), and no CHIPS funds had been received at quarter-end. The weighted share count has roughly tripled since 2022 ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/b-riley-cut-its-d-wave-target-to-27-its-still-168-above-the-stock-price)).

### 6. P/E ratio and what it implies
P/E is not meaningful (Yahoo forward -25.4). At about $3.74B market cap on about $13.2M of trailing revenue, QBTS trades near 285x trailing sales and about 77x EV on 2026 consensus (derived); TIKR put the forward sales multiple at about 66x on 2026-11-09, against a three-year average of 153x and a 2025 peak of 839x ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/d-wave-sinks-8-as-q3-loss-misses-and-quarterly-bookings-fall-19-after-quantum-rally)). Cash covers about 13% of the market cap (derived). Quantinuum at about 450x 2025 revenue (derived) shows the whole group is priced on long-dated outcomes; D-Wave is cheap only relative to its own bubble.

### Street view vs contrarian view
The Street is mostly Buy but cutting: consensus was $35 from 17 analysts in October, the lowest target $18.36, and the app shows $30.59 ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/b-riley-cut-its-d-wave-target-to-27-its-still-168-above-the-stock-price)). The contrarian bear case: a hype-cycle multiple on falling revenue, bookings concentrated in a few deals, tripled share count, a rival owning the foundry, and a lawsuit about the very deliveries Q4 depends on; short seller Kerrisdale called annealing a "commercial dead end" ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/25/04/45078865/d-wave-quantum-qbts-stock-falls-amid-valuation-critique-by-kerrisdale-capital), older than 6 months). The contrarian bull case: about three years of cash, more than 100 customers, a government equity partner that paid about $14.09 a share against a $9.84 price, RPO of $38.3M (from $2.9M) and a multiple already more than 60% below its own average. The government's higher entry price can be read as a strategic signal or simply as dilution struck at a better time.

### What would change the thesis
- Q4 2026 revenue against the roughly $32M implied by consensus, and whether any system revenue is recognized before FAU's early-2027 installation.
- Q4 bookings after Q3's $1.9M, and the pipeline claim (up more than 75% since Q2).
- Receipt of the initial CHIPS tranche (about $53.6M, secondary) and any new ATM or equity raise.
- Supply arrangements beyond SkyWater.
- Progress in the class action filed 2026-10-15.
- 17-qubit gate-model system delivery discussions for 2027.

## Conclusion

The tech sleeve's real exposure is one variable, AI infrastructure spending, seen from different points in the chain. Nvidia sells the chips, finances part of the demand, owns about 11.5% of CoreWeave and buys CoreWeave's unused capacity; CoreWeave and Oracle rent those chips with borrowed money to a short list of buyers led by OpenAI and Microsoft; and Micron and Everpure are both bets on memory prices staying high, in opposite ways (Micron earns the price, Everpure passes it through and holds prepaid NAND). Microsoft's November moves against neocloud and Oracle rentals hit two holdings in the same week, which is what correlated risk looks like in practice. A pitch that presents nine independent ideas would overstate the sleeve's diversification.

The second takeaway is that the P/E column misleads in both directions. The lowest multiples (Micron near 4x forward, Nvidia near 11x, Oracle at 11.5x on FY28) are the market declining to believe estimates at a possible peak or under financing strain, not bargains by default. The highest (Everpure at 151x, Duolingo at 52x, Palantir's various figures) are driven heavily by stock-based compensation and accounting basis. For a buy book, the more honest numbers per holding are the ones the market is watching: CoreWeave's secured borrowing rate against its 15.5% unsecured yield, Nvidia's receivables days, Oracle's rating and RPO, Micron's contract-price trend, Palantir's bookings, Duolingo's payers per daily user, Everpure's second-half cash flow, D-Wave's Q4 system revenue and WM Technology's use of its cash.

## 1. How does the company make money? (segments, OCI vs apps, RPO and OpenAI share)

### Takeaway
Oracle is now mostly a cloud company by revenue: Q1 FY27 cloud revenue was $11.6B of $19.3B total (60%), with OCI (infrastructure) at $7.4B (+121%) and growing much faster than SaaS apps at $4.2B (+10%), while legacy license and support fell 3%. RPO is $664B, and analysts (not Oracle) estimate about $300B of it, roughly half, is OpenAI.

## 6. P/E: trailing, forward, history vs peers, what it implies

### Takeaway
The ~20x trailing P/E is mostly explained by the ~50% price drop, plus a one-time $2.7B pre-tax Ampere gain in trailing earnings. On forward non-GAAP numbers Oracle trades at ~15.6x FY27 and ~11.5x FY28 consensus, near five-year lows and well below its 21x five-year average. ## 7. Contrarian angles (bear and bull)

Bear: Oracle is a leveraged, near-junk landlord to one customer (OpenAI) that is itself burning tens of billions a year, with GPU assets that age quickly (Microsoft's exit from older chips is an example). Bull: the stock trades near five-year low multiples after a ~50% drop, RPO is still growing, the Investor Day capped new funding and dated the FCF turn, and OpenAI's revenue and IPO prospects could re-rate the counterparty.
